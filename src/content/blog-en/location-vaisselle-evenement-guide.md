The choice of tableware affects service, handling and the return to washing. For a large event, counting the guests isn't enough: you need to look at the dishes, the drinks, the spaces and the pace at which people are served.

Reusable plastic, stainless steel, porcelain and glass each meet different constraints. The right mix is planned together with the teams who will use it.

## Start from the planned service

A buffet, a seated meal and catering spread across several stands don't use tableware at the same pace. List the items needed per service: plates, containers, cutlery, glassware, serving dishes and any accessories.

Note any planned changes of plates or glasses during the meal. If several services follow one another, check whether you are planning a complete set for each one or a washing rotation. This rotation must be confirmed in the schedule, never assumed to be available.

VIP areas, staff meals and stands open to the public may also have different needs.

## Choose materials according to their use

| Material | Points to check before choosing |
| --- | --- |
| Reusable plastic | Suitability for food and temperatures, drying, stacking, condition of the equipment |
| Stainless steel | Intended use, dimensions, handling, fit with how the meal is presented |
| Porcelain | Presentation, weight of the batches, breakage, packing and storage on site |
| Glass | Drinks served, venue restrictions, breakage, transport and storage |

This comparison is there to help you prepare your questions. It doesn't mean one material is always better than another. Check the supplier's documents and instructions, especially regarding heat and washing.

The [DGCCRF, the French consumer protection authority, sets out the conditions of use for materials in contact with food](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/les-regles-respecter-pour-les-materiaux-emballages-ustensiles-et-contenants-au-contact-des-aliments). Items must be used under the intended conditions, including when they are reusable.

## Size the batches with the people on the ground

Have your caterer or catering manager review the list. Confirm the number of guests, the services, the menus and the reserve needed. A number of place settings is no substitute for a detailed equipment list.

For sizes you aren't used to, ask for the dimensions or a sample. A plate listed in the catalogue may not suit how the dish is plated or the space available on the table.

Quantities then need to be confirmed against the stock available for your dates.

## Organise returns from the moment the tableware arrives

Decide where the empty packaging will be kept and where the dirty batches will be brought together. Give an instruction for removing waste and keeping the protective packaging needed for transport.

Clarify how cutlery, missing pieces and broken items are handled. The invoicing rules should be set out in the agreed conditions. A clear outgoing inventory and return report limit surprises after the event.

Washing and drying are operations to plan alongside the rental. Keep the clean circuit separate from the returns and confirm the handling equipment available.
