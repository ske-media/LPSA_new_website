Entrusting a service to a French ‘entreprise adaptée’ (a company employing mainly people with disabilities) combines an operational need with a purchase that supports the employment of people with disabilities. For some employers, it can also open up a deduction on the contribution linked to the obligation to employ disabled workers, known in France as the OETH.

This scheme calls for precise wording: it is a deduction from a contribution, subject to rules, not an automatic discount on the washing invoice.

## First check whether your company is concerned

In the private sector, the OETH applies to employers with at least 20 employees, according to the applicable headcount rules. Whether this threshold has been crossed for five consecutive calendar years is a point to look into. A contribution may be due when the obligation is not met in the ways provided for.

These guidelines are set out in detail by [Service Public Entreprendre](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/F39595), the French government's official information site for businesses. A SIREN number (French company registration number) and a headcount band found in a directory are not enough to establish your reporting situation. Your payroll department, your HR team or your adviser should confirm it.

Public employers come under a separate scheme, notably the FIPHFP (the fund for the integration of people with disabilities in the public service). The calculation shown here should not be applied to them automatically.

## Identify the eligible base within the service

The potential amount is calculated on the basis of 30% of the eligible base defined by the French Labour Code. This corresponds to the price excluding VAT, after deducting raw materials, products, materials, subcontracting, intermediate consumption and sales or marketing costs.

In other words, applying 30% to the total of an invoice for rental, transport and washing would be incorrect without first determining this base. The service invoiced and the eligible amount are not necessarily the same.

Ask the provider for the information and the certificate you need for your declaration. The usable amount is checked together with your other relevant purchases over the same period.

## Apply the cap at company level

The deduction is capped according to your employment rate of OETH beneficiaries: the cap is 50% of the gross contribution when this rate is below 3%, and 75% when it is at least 3%.

These caps apply to all contracts covered by the scheme, not to each provider separately. The [French Labour Code, article D5212-22](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/affichCodeArticle.do?categorieLien=cid&cidTexte=LEGITEXT000006072050&dateTexte=&idArticle=LEGIARTI000018495254), sets out the base and the limits. The deduction actually applied must also take into account the contribution still due and the rules that apply to your declaration.

## Read an example without mistaking it for your own result

Fictional example: the certified eligible base is €5,000 excl. VAT. The potential deduction linked to this purchase is €1,500.

If the gross contribution used to calculate the cap is €4,000 and the employment rate is below 3%, the shared cap is €2,000. If other contracts have already used €1,200 of this cap, there is €800 of headroom left under this heading. The potential €1,500 could therefore not be used in full in this example, and the final result also depends on the full calculation of the contribution.
