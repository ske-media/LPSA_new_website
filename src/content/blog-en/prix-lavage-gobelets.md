The price of cup washing only becomes meaningful once you know what it covers. A unit price can look attractive while leaving out drying, counting, packaging or travel. For a large event, the right budget is the one for the complete service, right up to the next time the cups are used.

## Compare services that go to the same point

Ask each provider to state exactly which operations are included: receipt, any sorting, washing, drying, checking, counting and packing. Also check the rule that applies to cups that are very dirty, damaged or mixed in with other models.

A quote for washing and a quote for rental with washing don't cover the same need. In the second case, the price also covers making a cup stock available and the conditions for returning it. The quantity rented, the quantity washed and the quantity missing may appear as separate lines.

La Plonge s’Adapte prices transport separately. This makes it easy to see what difference an organised collection, or a drop-off by your own team, makes.

## What makes a quote vary

The overall volume matters, but so does the way it is split. A single uniform batch of 3,000 cups and ten separate jobs of 300 pieces create different needs for receipt, sorting, handling and transport.

Sizes should be listed in detail. A 25 cl cup, a 50 cl model and a jug are not interchangeable units in a quote. The return schedule, how often rotations happen and how well the crates are prepared also affect the organisation.

For an ongoing relationship, give the annual volume you have in mind and any dates you already know. A defined commitment can be looked at as part of a framework agreement; it should set out its conditions, not just announce an optimistic total.

## Build a clear budget

Use this structure:

**Total budget = washing per size + packaging not included + transport + any additional services agreed.**

Purely illustrative example: 2,000 washes at €0.15 excl. VAT come to €300 excl. VAT before transport and any extras. This calculation explains the method; it is neither a national average nor a price offer from La Plonge s’Adapte.

To compare two proposals, divide their total cost by the volume actually handled, with the same scope and the same tax basis. A forgotten transport line can change the comparison on a small batch; on the other hand, a grouped round can make it easier to organise several events.

## Compare with washing in-house too

Washing done by volunteers or staff takes time, a machine, consumables, space for drying and someone responsible for the result. You also need to allow for redoing batches that are badly dried or badly prepared.

Write these resources down even if they don't lead to a specific invoice. The aim isn't to put an artificial price on every volunteer minute, but to see which tasks your team is genuinely willing to take on after the event.

What an external service mainly brings is an agreed scope and schedule. Whether it makes sense depends on the full cost and on the organisation it makes possible.

## Send the right information from the very first contact

To get a proposal you can actually use, send the quantities per size, the town, the dates, your transport needs and the date you need the cups back. If the figures are still estimates, give a range and say what will be confirmed later.
