Buying a cup stock gives you control over the equipment. Renting avoids tying up stock between two events. Both options can work; which one suits you depends on the number of events, the quantities actually reused and your ability to organise everything that happens afterwards.

To choose, look at the cost and running of the cup stock over a whole year, not just the price of the first order.

## Buying suits a cup stock that really circulates

A recurring organiser may benefit from owning consistent sizes, suited to their drinks and serving points. But a stock you buy will need to be stored, inventoried, washed and gradually replaced.

Customisation deserves the same thought. A design tied to a single edition can limit future use. A lasting identity, with no date, can make it easier to reuse the cups across several events, if your communication allows it.

Before buying, ask for samples and check the intended food uses, temperature limits, compatibility with professional washing and storage conditions. If you want cups made in Europe, ask for documented proof of origin: the seller's address alone doesn't prove where the cups were made.

La Plonge s’Adapte can help you choose a model and plan how it will be washed. The aim is to find a cup suited to your service and to future cycles, rather than one chosen only for its print.

## Rental meets one-off or variable needs

Rental lets you adjust the cup stock to a particular event or top up an existing stock. It can make sense when your needs vary a lot from one edition to the next, or when you have little storage space.

Check the sizes and quantities available, the dates the cups are provided, the return conditions, the planned washing and the rules for losses or damage. Availability must be confirmed; a capacity announced by a partner does not count as a booking for your dates.

For a multi-day event, [sizing your cup stock](/en/blog/combien-gobelets-prevoir-evenement/) is still essential, even when renting. You need to tell apart what will be in service and what will be waiting to go back for washing.

## Compare costs over the same period

For buying, add up:

- purchase and customisation of the cup stock;
- washing at each rotation;
- transport, storage and handling;
- replacement of pieces that are lost or no longer fit for use.

For rental, add up the rental fees, washing, transport and any charges set out in the contract. Then compare these budgets over the same season and with similar quantities.

A comparison that forgets the storage of a stock you own, or the washing of a rental, won't help you decide. Use a cautious forecast of reuse: events you hope for but haven't confirmed don't yet count as a rotation.

## A mixed approach can be more flexible

You can own a cup stock that matches your usual needs and rent extra for an exceptional peak. This approach means keeping the batches clearly identified and defining how each one is returned.

Across a wider area, a [shared cup stock run by a local authority](/en/blog/parc-gobelets-mutualise-collectivite/) can also serve several events. It requires a common set of rules for bookings, washing and handling discrepancies.

## Three questions to help you decide

How many times will you actually use the cup stock this year? Who will take care of storing it and doing the inventories? Which washing schedule guarantees the stock you need for your next dates?

If the answers are clear, the financial choice becomes easier to read. If they are still open, plan the organisation with your provider before ordering a large number of pieces.
