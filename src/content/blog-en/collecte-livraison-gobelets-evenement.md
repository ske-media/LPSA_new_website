"Do we need to bring you the cups?" This is often the first question when an organiser discovers outsourced cup washing. The answer depends on the set-up you choose: you can arrange a drop-off and pick-up yourself, or have the collection and delivery organised for you.

La Plonge s’Adapte offers this service with transport partners. Transport is priced separately and the arrangements are agreed in the proposal.

## Describe what actually needs to be transported

A total number of cups isn't enough to describe a pick-up. We need to know the sizes, the number of crates or boxes, their dimensions, their weight and whether there are pallets.

Say who supplies the transport containers and whether they are also used to bring the clean cups back. Packaging must suit the planned circuit, the handling involved and the provider's instructions. A crate that is filled differently from one batch to the next can throw off an estimate based on the number of crates.

If you don't have this information yet, just say so. The first conversation can be used precisely to work out the packaging required, before booking the transport.

## Check access and handling

Give the exact address, the access point and any site restrictions: pedestrian street, barriers, permitted times, loading bay, floor level or distance between the stock and the vehicle.

Say what equipment is available. Having pallets doesn't mean a pallet truck, a tail lift or a forklift will automatically be provided. Likewise, identify who is responsible for loading and unloading.

An on-site contact who can be reached during the agreed slot avoids a carrier waiting at a locked gate while the coordinator deals with another emergency.

## Prepare the collection without washing twice

Follow the agreed instructions for emptying drinks, removing waste, separating batches and closing or arranging the containers. The washing will be done by the provider, so there's no need to improvise a different treatment from the one planned.

Label each batch with the event, the date and the sizes. Add a delivery note that distinguishes counted quantities from estimates. Keep a copy when the cups leave and agree how any discrepancies will be handled.

For an event over several days, the collection area must stay separate from the clean stock. This separation makes restocking easy to follow for the teams receiving the returns.

## Coordinate the journey with the washing turnaround

The schedule that matters starts at pick-up and ends when the cups are available again. It includes the outbound journey, the processing and the return journey. A late departure time can change the return slot available.

Tell us your real deadline and the quantity you need by that date. If part of the stock can come back later, that flexibility can be looked at. For several events close together, grouping them can also be considered, provided the batches and schedules are compatible.

## Choose between a drop-off and an organised collection

A drop-off by your own team can work if you have a suitable vehicle and a journey already planned. Do, however, factor driving time, handling and opening hours into your comparison.

An organised collection lets you set up this flow together with your washing service. Ask for the quote to specify the addresses, the number of journeys, the time slots and the handling services included.
